Flagship Project · Weeks 3–8

FreshLens

The FreshLens target is simple: point your camera at a fridge, basket, or produce shelf and score every item in one scene. Then turn the advisory result into action: rescue what’s turning, or mark it down before it’s lost. The real YOLO/live-service path is not yet reproducible in a clean checkout.

This summer the team builds one feature on top: Snap-to-Claim — a capped, evidence-backed, human-in-the-loop refund loop for perishables that arrive spoiled.

Python FastAPI PyTorch YOLO / Ultralytics Claude API JavaScript

The leap

Expired Solutions already scans one item at a time and scores its freshness 0–100 across 135 produce types. FreshLens is the 10x target: scan the whole scene at once. The prototype and offline pieces exist; reproducible real-detector/live-service proof remains open.

That single capability serves both sides of the business. Pointed at a shopper’s fridge, it’s a magic “what do I eat first, and what can I cook tonight?” moment. Pointed at a store’s produce section, it’s an instant freshness audit that tells the manager exactly what to mark down—the heart of the markdown-automation pitch.

This summer’s build: Snap-to-Claim

On top of the FreshLens base, the team builds one focused feature for Weeks 3–8: Snap-to-Claim (Return Trust) — a capped, evidence-backed, human-in-the-loop refund loop for perishables that arrive spoiled.

A shopper let down by a discounted perishable snaps the item + receipt in-app. The Expired Solutions freshness model advises on condition. A capped policy decides, with auto-approval disabled under the current calibration gate. The intended result is duplicate-checked, bound to a provenance receipt, and written to a tamper-evident audit trail. The wedge is not the freshness score; it is the closed, verifiable-evidence loop.

1

Advise

The model scores the photo 0–100 on the canonical scale (0 = fresh, 100 = spoiled/urgent). It’s an advisory input, never a guarantee — copy never says “our AI confirms this is spoiled”.

2

Decide (capped)

The target policy can auto-approve only a calibrated narrow band. The current RE-SCOPE gate sends every case to a human. A rolling $15 / 30-day and $10 / claim ceiling limits exposure; the policy never auto-declines.

3

Prove

The local candidate now creates one immutable signed decision artifact and verifies its mock-anchor state. Durable storage, external trust, and money issuance remain separate blocked capabilities.

Four guardrails are non-negotiable: advisory language only · human-in-the-loop outside the narrow band · calibration before any auto-threshold · cap + duplicate checks before the model. Full detail in INITIATIVE-freshness-guarantee.md and the executable SPEC-claim-loop.md.

Target claim flow

1 · SnapShopper photographs the item + receipt in-app.
→
2 · CheckCap + duplicate checks run first (before the model).
→
3 · AdviseModel scores condition (0 = fresh → 100 = urgent).
→
4 · DecideThe current gate routes every claim to a separately authorized human reviewer.
→
5 · ProveThe local prototype binds the decision, verifies its mock anchor, and exports an account-scoped report.

Mind the direction. The canonical FreshLens scale is 0 = perfectly fresh, 100 = spoiled / urgent (low = green/fresh, high = red/use-now). The raw model returns the opposite (high = fresh); the es_api adapter inverts it at the seam, so all claim code works on the canonical scale.

Target architecture

A FastAPI service that puts a capped, audited policy around the freshness model. The current local candidate remains human-review-only and verifies the receipt-binding/mock-anchor boundary; it is not a durable production or issuance architecture.
