Weeks 9–11 · refrigerator inventory pivot

One guided sweep. Every visible item. One honest review.

The team is moving from a single-item scanner and claim workflow to a multi-capture inventory system: cover the fridge, segment and track physical items, identify or abstain, score only eligible visible condition, reconcile receipts, and let the shopper confirm the result.

The system we are defining

Identity, visible condition, purchase provenance, and user confirmation are different signals. The architecture keeps them separate so uncertainty is visible and corrections remain cheap.

01

Guide coverage

Shelf, door, and drawer prompts collect only the views needed. Blur, glare, and missing regions trigger focused reshoots.

02

Find instances

Per-frame masks become stable tracks. Cross-view deduplication prevents one apple or carton becoming multiple inventory rows.

03

Identify or abstain

A versioned taxonomy returns alternatives and OOD reasons. Unknown items stay unknown instead of being forced into the nearest class.

04

Score eligible evidence

Visible-condition scoring runs only on supported, adequately visible produce. It does not claim safety, expiry, or internal quality.

05

Reconcile receipts

Synthetic receipt lines can support identity, quantity, and purchase-time provenance without inventing opened or expiry dates.

06

Review once

One consolidated screen supports edit, merge, split, remove, and add before confirmed inventory is persisted.

07

Measure the journey

Coverage recall, count error, duplicate rate, OOD false accepts, correction rate, latency, cost, and time-to-value share one candidate SHA.

08

Prove the release

Source, model, device, backend, TestFlight, App Store, website, and production claims remain separate evidence gates.

Three-week delivery cadence

Contracts lead implementation. Every lane produces a consumer example, a negative test, an artifact digest, and an independent break attempt.

WEEK 9 · AUG 10–14

Audit, expectations, baseline

Retain the extension's findings-first rule. Each lane defines “healthy,” its metric and proving gate, then freezes schemas and synthetic 1/5/15/30-item adversarial fixtures. The old snapshot is a labeled baseline only.

WEEK 10 · AUG 17–21

Build slices and maintainers

Join capture through review and build one propose-only maintainer per frozen gate. Every loop honors the kill flag, cannot merge or deploy, and needs 48 hours of unattended evidence.

WEEK 11 · AUG 24–28

Umbrella, stress, handoff

Join registry read, scoped dispatch, evidence aggregation, and a local digest. Inject a synthetic regression, produce a proposal, rerun the gate, and transfer only owner-reviewed, source-bound work.

Automation continuity: the extension plan remains part of the pivot, but its generic backlog is repartitioned into the eight product lanes below. Maintainers propose; people review. Automatic merge, deployment, settings changes, production mutation, and external digest delivery remain owner-gated.

Eight owners, four review pairs

Ownership is bounded by a single interface. Reviewers attack the named failure path; they do not share write scope.

Access is broad enough to build, narrow enough to stay safe

Interns can

  • Branch, push, open pull requests, and peer review in the sandbox and hub.
  • Use synthetic fixtures and approved public references.
  • Build offline interfaces, tests, scorecards, and sanitized artifacts.
  • Request a bounded capability with schema, cost, retention, and failure contracts.

Interns cannot

  • Access the canonical product repository, customer media, inboxes, raw training data, weights, or production logs.
  • Deploy, change infrastructure, sign builds, upload TestFlight, or submit to the App Store.
  • Treat photo capture as training consent or broad inbox access as a shortcut.
  • Call an old scanner, model, provider path, or local test current release evidence.
