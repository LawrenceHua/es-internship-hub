How a one-founder product company runs itself.

This is a walkthrough of Xpired, a produce-tracking app built and run by one person with a set of AI agents and automations, and of the automations themselves: what fires on its own, what a person still has to sign off on, and how you could set up something like it for your own product.

Xpired in 60 seconds

Honest about where it stands today, not where the roadmap says it will be.

The one shipping AI feature is fridge photo enumeration. POST /api/v1/fridge/enumerate sends a photo to a vision provider and gets back a list of items: it identifies what is in the fridge. It does not score freshness, and that score has never shipped.
No in-house ML model ships in the app. There is no .mlmodel bundled in the iOS app, and the production backend has no custom model loaded either (checked live, see "What is true today" below). What the app calls a freshness scanner is a target, not a shipped feature: AppConfiguration.swift gates scannerEnabled behind #if DEBUG && SCANNER_LAB, so a normal build cannot reach it.
It is in beta, in App Store review, with zero public users. The next build is submitted and waiting for Apple's review; it is being distributed to TestFlight's internal group and its external public-link group today. Anyone in the room can join the public beta and try it: testflight.apple.com/join/74ZbJPm6.

The automations

Every automation on the full inventory has a job, a boundary, and a status label; the boundary matters as much as the job. Three of what runs unattended:

Release: build once, canary, promote. fly-deploy.yml builds a single image, checks a canary with a signed probe, and promotes that exact same digest; it never rebuilds between canary and production, and only the owner can dispatch it.
Watch: a daily steward, one Slack channel. A daily steward watches quality, storage, deployment, and activity and posts what it finds to the team's Slack channel every morning; a dead-man heartbeat watches the steward itself. Neither one applies its own fix: findings become drafts, and a person decides.
Triage: shopper feedback becomes a morning queue. A daily digest pulls unreviewed shopper feedback and scan corrections from production and posts the queue to Slack; from there the routing is human, digest to owner brief to pull request. It never opens a pull request or applies a correction by itself.
