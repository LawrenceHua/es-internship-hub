A day of a change

A change starts as one human sentence. It earns its way forward by leaving something another person can inspect at every handoff.

VERIFIED

Eleven stations, eleven records

This is the ordinary route described by the operating records. The arrows show a chain of custody, not an automatic promise that a change will ship.

01

An owner sentence

An owner says what they want in ordinary language. The planner preserves the request before turning it into work.

Produces: a brief line, recorded verbatim.

02

A shared mailbox entry

The planner records the work contract and the path that must later be checked from the stated revision.

Produces: an es-ops/briefs/QUEUE.md entry.

03

A bounded brief

The brief names the facts, deliverables, decisive command, a mutation that must fail, limits on network and secrets, and a budget.

Produces: a named brief such as 20260901-B0-kpi-truth-and-dead-plists.md.

04

An agent lane

A detached launcher starts one bounded lane and leaves enough evidence to find it again. Concurrency limits are recorded before more lanes start.

Produces: .pid and .launch.log records.

05

A report and claim ledger

The builder records literal command output and labels every claim so a reader can separate proof from an open question.

Produces: reports/<TAG>.md.

06

Independent verification

The planner re-runs the decisive command in a fresh shell on the stated commit. A read-only reviewer then tries to refute the claims from a separate worktree.

Produces: es-ops/briefs/QUEUE.md: reviewer verdict.

07

Pull-request gates

The protected release branch requires release contracts, external-review gate tests, and shopper tests. Disabled workflow files remain as tombstones and nothing deploys simply because code was pushed.

Produces: a required-check result from .github/workflows/release-contracts.yml.

08

Merge with a re-check

A merge follows a reviewer MERGE verdict re-run by the planner. The merge revision is then checked again rather than assumed good from its earlier branch result.

Produces: es-ops/briefs/QUEUE.md: merge SHA + ci-verify result.

09

Human release readiness

When a release is due, a human dispatches the protected workflow for the pinned build after the pre-submission check passes. This is a choice, not a push trigger.

Produces: a manual workflow dispatch and receipt from .github/workflows/shopper-release.yml.

10

Review verdict watch

A watcher reads the external review state on an interval so a person does not have to refresh a page and guess whether anything changed.

Produces: state/es-asc-verdict-latest.json.

11

The release desk

The planner-maintained desk gathers review hours, pull request states, and the owner’s next click into one status page.

Produces: state/xpired-release-desk.html.

Notice that every arrow ends in a record a skeptical reader can re-check before trusting the next station.

INFERRED

The teaching point

Every handoff in this chain produces an artifact a skeptic can re-check: the brief, the report, the claim ledger, the workflow run, and the receipt. The system is not an AI that does things. It is a paper trail that happens to be written by machines.
