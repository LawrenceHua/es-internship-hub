Provenance Receipts

The merged local prototype turns a claim decision into a signed, tamper-evident evidence receipt and verifies a mock anchor. Durable multi-worker storage, independent external trust, and money issuance remain BLOCKED; a public anchor is a future research option, not a Week 7 commitment.

The pain point

Perishable claims need a trustworthy evidence trail without treating a model score as a guarantee or a recorded approval as proof that money moved.

With loose produce, a barcode identifies the category, not the exact unit, store, time, or condition. Evidence can be reused or mismatched, and the physical item can be substituted. The product question is therefore not merely “can we sign a hash?” It is whether authorization, caps, duplicates, evidence binding, human review, recovery, and audit history remain coherent together.

How it works

The local evidence system hashes, signs, binds, and verifies one decision artifact. Its mock anchor proves the local interface only; it does not provide independent external trust.

1

Hash

SHA-256 of the photo plus the scan facts—store, lot code, UTC time, freshness score, items—into one canonical fingerprint.

2

Sign

Sign the fingerprint so later edits are detectable. HMAC is the current compatibility path; Ed25519 is conditional on a frozen key-identity, rotation, and dependency contract.

3

Anchor

Verify the 32-byte fingerprint against the merged mock-anchor contract. Public anchoring requires durability, key management, authorization, independent verification, and a separate release decision.

4

Decide

At verification time, re-hash the artifact, check its signature and mock-anchor state, then apply bounded policy inputs before an assigned human reviewer records a decision.

A simple tamper attempt fails cleanly: edit the freshness score or swap the store on a saved receipt and the fingerprint no longer matches, so verification returns digest_mismatch. The harder problem is physical substitution: proving the presented item is the same item. That needs policy and additional signals, not merely a signed hash.

What it proves — and what it doesn’t

It proves locally: the verifier can recompute the same decision-artifact digest, verify the current compatibility signature, match the mock-anchor intent, and reject a mutated artifact.

It does not prove durable or independent existence, time, or issuer; physical-item authenticity; model correctness; current detector quality; external anchoring; or issuance. The mock anchor and process-local state cannot substitute for those gates.

The competitive product is the decision layer: require the original purchase receipt, store and lot match, claim-window check, account risk, a current evidence photo, and a freshness-delta comparison. A future external anchor may make silent edits and backdating harder; it does not replace store operations or human authorization.

External trust remains blocked: any future public anchor would require privacy review, durable state, key management, recovery, independent verification, and explicit release authority. No personal data may be published.

Architecture

The merged FastAPI prototype signs a decision artifact and verifies its fingerprint through a local mock anchor. The current closeout plan keeps durable storage and public anchoring blocked behind separate prerequisites and release authority.

The local artifact is useful for binding and tamper checks in one process. It does not yet survive multi-worker restart, prove independent existence, or authorize issuance. Public anchoring becomes relevant only after durable state, key management, recovery, and an independently testable external-trust contract exist.

Beyond the current claim prototype

A future durable, independently trusted, lot-linked receipt could support other operations. These are research directions, not current release claims.

Recall management

Find every receipt carrying a recalled lot_code — a targeted recall instead of a blanket category pull.

Chain of custody

Each supplier→store handoff issues a signed condition receipt; “arrived already bad” disputes arbitrate from evidence.

Shrink & markdown audit

A signed markdown event (item, photo, manager, time) deters markdown fraud and feeds loss analysis.

Claim risk signals

Repeated claims, stale receipts, mismatched stores, and impossible freshness deltas can route evidence to assigned human review. Automatic approval and issuance remain disabled.

Insurance claims

Timestamped, photo-backed condition records make spoilage claims faster and harder to abuse.

Loyalty & transparency

“This lot received here on the 12th, in good condition” — a freshness story a shopper can scan.

FSMA 204 alignment

The lot_code is the same Traceability Lot Code the FDA food-traceability rule mandates upstream (compliance ~2028).

How this reference fits the closeout

This is not an assigned Week 7 feature lane. Use it to understand the merged local binding/mock-anchor boundary. Follow the proposed action plan for current work; external anchoring stays BLOCKED until its durability and trust prerequisites have their own evidence and release decision.
